**Shape:** Free download plus one-time Pro unlock (license key), sold direct through Polar as merchant of record. Optional one-time cosmetic packs. Steam listing later as a second channel.

### Free vs paid
**Rule:** Free = everything that works on the one PC in front of you. Pro = anything that reaches across devices or remembers your work over time (phone, friends, workspaces, Claude Code). The free pill already feels complete as a Dynamic Island plus Control Center; the paid half is what a person wants after a week of use.

Free:
- Pill, morphing sizes, any edge, Peek behind
- Games FPS and ping
- Music player
- Control Center, setup, tiles
- Timers, reminders, calendar, weather, downloads, screenshots, clipboard, system stats
- Call mic and camera buttons, Do not disturb
- Bot with offline Local AI
- Land on same network and QR to phones
- Self-update

Paid:
- Island on your phone
- Land anywhere (friend codes)
- Workspaces, Notice, Time tab

### Teens, students, launch
- **Teens without a card:** Polar checkout takes cards and wallets; most 13-17 year-olds use a parent's card, a teen debit card (for example Apple Pay/Google Pay from a teen bank account), or a gift: Pro has a "Buy as a gift" link so a parent or friend pays and sends the key by email. Steam channel (later) lets teens pay with Steam Wallet gift cards bought in stores; Steam accounts require age 13 (unverified). PayPal support on Polar is unverified. Selling to minors: no rule found in the research budget; contracts with minors in Quebec can be annulled (Civil Code, unverified). Mitigation: terms say under 18 needs a parent's OK, and a 30-day no-questions refund.
- **Students:** Student price $7.99 USD / CA$10.99 (about 38% off) with a code. Verify with a school email (.edu, .ca CEGEP/university domain) or a teacher's email for under-18s; code issued by Moss manually at first. Also: a "gift to a friend" 2-pack at $22.
- **Launch offer:** First 14 days or first 500 buyers: $8.99 USD / CA$11.99 ("founder price"), then $12.99 for good. Early buyers also get a free first theme pack. Time-limited to avoid training people to wait; no repeated sales in year one except one in the back-to-school window.

### Money per sale
At $12.99 USD on Polar: fee 5% = $0.65 + $0.50 = $1.15, plus 1.5% non-US card = $0.19, so Moss keeps about $11.65 (90%). Buyer tax is added on top and remitted by Polar. Student $7.99: 0.40 + 0.50 + 0.12 = $1.02 fee, keeps about $6.97. Launch $8.99: fee about $1.09, keeps about $7.90. On Steam (later) $12.99: 30% = $3.90, keeps about $9.09 before Steam's tax handling (unverified). Moss still pays Canadian income tax on net; GST/QST registration rules not researched.

### Why this model
- Island's direct rivals set a clear price band: Alcove $14.99, DynamicLake $13.99, NotchNook $15, NotchPixel $9.99, all one-time-friendly. $12.99 sits inside it.
- A free download that already works lets 13-28 year-olds try it without a card and spread it through desk-setup posts; Bongo Cat and Desktop Mate show young Steam audiences pay for extras to something free.
- No subscription fits Island's cost structure: the phone link and Land use free n0 relays and Moss pays nothing for AI, so a lifetime unlock has almost no ongoing cost per user.
- The free/paid line is natural: the pill and Control Center work on one PC; phone, friends, Workspaces and Claude Code are the parts that reach across devices and grow with use.
- Direct sale through a merchant of record keeps about 90% per sale and removes all sales-tax and VAT work for a student seller, with Steam and the Microsoft Store left open as later channels.

### Risks
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Free download is a feature on the cheap side; free DynamicWin-style apps exist, so Pro conversion may be well under 1%. | Keep the free pill genuinely good so it spreads; make Pro features (phone, Land anywhere, Workspaces) visible as locked tiles with a one-click try. |
| Unsigned exe: SmartScreen scares 13-17 year-olds and people paying money. | Sign with Azure Artifact Signing ($120/year) before taking any payment; no paid launch unsigned. |
| Pay once means no recurring income for relay, Vercel and support costs as users grow. | Phone link runs end-to-end through public n0 relays, so cost per user stays near $0; set a rule to re-price Pro for new buyers or sell a v2 license if relay costs appear. |
| License keys get shared or cracked; the app is MIT history and Tauri is easy to patch. | Accept some leakage; 3-device limit, signed offline keys, no heavy DRM. Teens who pirate were never customers. |
| Private GitHub repo breaks self-update for everyone who downloads. | Publish builds to a public releases-only repo or cheap object storage and point the updater there. |
| Teen buyers without cards, plus Quebec minor-contract rules. | Gift link for parents, Steam Wallet channel later, 30-day refund, terms asking for a parent's OK under 18. |
| Free services (Open-Meteo, Vercel Hobby, n0 relays, ntfy) forbid or limit commercial use. | Read each term before launch; swap weather source, move phone app to a commercial-friendly host, budget for n0 paid relay. |
| Mac app is unnotarized and has fewer features, so Mac buyers pay for less. | State plainly which Pro features exist on Mac; offer refunds; pay $99 Apple fee before charging Mac users. |
| Merchant of record may reject or hold a solo, student, Canadian seller. | Apply to Polar and Paddle in parallel; start with Polar's free Starter plan (no monthly cost). |

### Shapes rejected
- Paid upfront (Wallpaper Engine style): nobody can try it, SmartScreen plus a price plus no trial kills a 13-17 audience; Wallpaper Engine only works because it has Steam's trust and about $5.
- Pay-what-you-want with a minimum: Gumroad-style PWYW converts worse than a fixed price for a tool with a clear Pro line, and the 10% + 50c fee is higher (Gumroad fees source).
- Donations only (Rainmeter style): Rainmeter is free MIT donationware; no source shows it funds a developer, and Moss wants to charge.
- Free base with only cosmetic purchases (Bongo Cat / Desktop Mate): works for companion toys, but Island's value is utility, so cosmetics alone would earn little.

### Why this model
- The Plus features (phone from anywhere, Land to friends, sync, Workspaces memory) cross devices and networks, so charging every year is easier to explain than charging for a pill on one PC.
- The audience is 13-28 and cash-poor; $24/year is $2/month, below NotchNook's $3/month list price and in line with its $2/month Oct 2026 price, and the student plan is $12/year.
- Moss has real yearly costs of about $700 (Vercel, weather, Apple, domain), so income must recur or it can stop paying for the service.
- A complete free tier keeps Island competitive with free tools and gives word of mouth among gamers and desk-setup people; only Plus users carry the cost.
- The Microsoft Store removes the SmartScreen warning, handles tax, takes gift cards and Family accounts for teens, and ends the public-GitHub updater problem, all for 15%.

### Risks
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Subscriptions for a desktop utility are disliked by the audience, and free rivals (DynamicWin, PowerToys, Rainmeter) exist | Keep the free tier complete on one PC, sell only cross-device and memory features, default to yearly at $24, and show the lifetime option in the FAQ. |
| The ongoing value is thin: the app runs locally and Moss pays nothing for AI. Real ongoing costs are Vercel ($240), weather ($348), signing and relay dependence, about $700 a year | State plainly on the store page that Plus pays for the phone service, updates and sync. If the phone link ever stays free of server cost, drop the price rather than keep it. |
| Gating phone features is client-side only, because the link is peer-to-peer (iroh) with no Moss server; a modified build could bypass it | Accept leakage at this scale; use signed license tokens verified offline and do not spend hours on anti-piracy. |
| Third-party free services (Open-Meteo, n0 relays, ntfy.sh, Vercel Hobby) may block commercial use or rate-limit | Buy Open-Meteo Standard ($29/month) or swap provider, move to Vercel Pro, read the n0 and ntfy terms before launch, and keep a self-hosted ntfy as backup. |
| Monthly price of $2.99 loses about 22% to Paddle's 50 cents | Push yearly as the default and sell monthly mainly through the Microsoft Store (15%). |
| Teens without cards cannot subscribe | Sell on the Microsoft Store (gift cards, Family accounts), add a pay-by-link for parents, and offer student pricing. |
| Unsigned exe and unnotarized Mac app hurt trust and conversion | Ship Windows through the Store first; get Azure Artifact Signing and the Apple program ($99) before the direct downloads open. |
| Private GitHub repo breaks the self-updater for users | Publish release files from a public releases-only repo or a CDN; the Store build updates through the Store. |
| Selling to under-18s: unknown legal rules for minors in Quebec | 14-day no-questions refund, 13+ only, ask a lawyer or Quebec's consumer office before launch (not researched). |
| Tax: if Moss sells outside a merchant of record he owes GST/QST and foreign VAT | Never use the 0% own-commerce option; only sell through Microsoft commerce and Paddle, which collect the tax. |

### Shapes rejected
- Pay once (the other researcher's lane): it fits desktop utilities, but Moss has yearly costs of about $700 that never stop, so a one-time price can run out of money.
- Lifetime as the main offer: NotchNook and Alcove show buyers expect about $15 once; a lifetime-first price caps income per user and leaves nothing to cover the yearly costs. Kept only as a $59 side option.
- Donations or supporter-only tier: Rainmeter-style donations (unverified) earn little at 13-28 and a solo developer cannot count on them.
- Cosmetics (Bongo Cat style paid packs): Bongo Cat sells $5-10 packs on Steam (see source), but Island is a utility, not a pet, and skins don't justify Moss's ongoing costs.
- Family or multi-PC as the main plan: the audience is teens and students, not households. Folded into Plus as '3 PCs + 2 Macs per subscription' instead.
- Steam as the channel: 30% cut and a $100 fee per app (see source); Steam is built for one-time game sales and I found no confirmation of subscription support for software (unverified).
